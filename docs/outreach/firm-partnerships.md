# Firm & school partnerships

This is the highest-value channel and the one Gaurav can run solo while Krish is
away. It's also the answer to the hardest problem: **getting senior tradespeople
on the platform.** Reddit brings juniors. Firms and schools bring mentors.

## A note on the contact list

**I have not fabricated any contact details.** Inventing plausible-looking phone
numbers and emails for real businesses would have produced a list that looks
finished and wastes your time on dead numbers — or worse, cold-calls the wrong
people.

What follows is verified starting points plus a repeatable method. Fill
[tracker.csv](tracker.csv) as you go.

## Building the firm list (30 minutes, repeatable)

For each of `plumber`, `HVAC contractor`, `electrician` × each target town
(Newark, Jersey City, Edison, Trenton, Paterson, Elizabeth):

1. **Google Maps** the trade + town. Filter to 15+ reviews — a proxy for an
   established shop with staff, which means apprentices.
2. Take the name, phone, and website from the listing. Get a **named person** from
   the website's About page where possible; "Hi, is the owner there?" converts far
   worse than "Hi, is Mike available?"
3. Prefer shops advertising **"apprentice wanted"** or **"now hiring"** — they have
   an active training need, which is the reason to care about us.
4. Log it in `tracker.csv`. Aim for 40–50 before starting calls, so a bad run of
   ten doesn't feel like failure.

Skip: national franchises (no local decision-maker), one-person operations (no
apprentices), and anyone whose site looks abandoned.

## Phone script — Gaurav

Best window: **7:30–8:30am or 4–5:30pm**. Mid-morning is when they're on a job.

> Hi, is this [name]? My name's Gaurav Kohli — I'm not selling anything, I'll be
> ninety seconds.
>
> My son is seventeen and he's built a free community site for people starting out
> in the trades — plumbing, HVAC, electrical. The idea is that apprentices can ask
> questions and get answers from people who've actually done the work, instead of
> guessing or watching YouTube.
>
> I'm calling shops like yours for two reasons. If you've got apprentices, it's a
> free place for them to get answers. And if you'd ever be willing to answer the
> odd question yourself, that's the part that makes it work.
>
> Would it be alright if I sent you the link to look at?

**If yes:** get the email, send within the hour (template below), log it.
**If "what's the catch":** *"There isn't one — it's free, there's nothing to buy,
and we don't sell leads. He's a high school student building something useful."*
**If "I'm busy":** *"Understood — can I email it instead?"* Then hang up. Don't push.
**If no:** thank them and move on. Note it in the tracker; don't call back.

**Lead with Krish being seventeen.** It's the reason the call doesn't sound like a
sales call, and it's why people stay on the line. The review flagged age as a
possible bias to offset — in practice it's the hook, as long as an adult is the
one making the call.

> 2. Frankly, whether you think it's useful at all — your feedback would shape it

>

>
> Gaurav Kohli

**Question 2 is not a courtesy.** An association director who has run
apprenticeships for twenty years will tell you in one sentence whether this is
useful — worth more than fifty signups.

## Targets

| Metric | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Firms researched into the tracker | 40–50 |
| Calls attempted | 20–25 |
| Conversations (actually spoke to a decision-maker) | 8–12 |
| Links sent | 6–10 |
| Senior pros who sign up and post once | **3–5** |

That last row is the one that matters. Five genuinely active seniors makes the
community real; five hundred juniors with nobody answering does not.

## Prepare for these three questions

1. **"Is my licence exposed if I give advice on there?"** — Real concern, honest
   answer: general advice isn't a licensed service, we don't verify licences, and
   nobody's hiring anybody through the platform. Also worth telling Krish: the
   project brief flags that legal counsel should review terms of service and
   liability. That's still outstanding and this question is why it matters.
2. **"What do you do with my data?"** — Nothing. No ads, no selling, no reselling.
3. **"What's in it for you?"** — Nothing financial. It's a school project that
   Krish wants to be genuinely used. Say that plainly; anything cagier reads as
   evasion.
