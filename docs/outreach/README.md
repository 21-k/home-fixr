## The constraint that shapes all of this

Krish is at a three-week UPenn program and busier until August. So the split is:

**Gaurav can do solo now**
- Firm and association calls (see `firm-partnerships.md`) — this is also where his
  involvement genuinely helps, since a 30-year plumber takes a call from another
  adult more readily than from a high-schooler

**Needs Krish, queue for August**
- Reddit posting. This *must* come from him personally — see below

**Do not batch-schedule Reddit posts to go out while nobody can reply.** An
unanswered thread reads as drive-by marketing and is the fastest way to get the
domain blacklisted.

## The one strategic point worth internalising

Krish being 17 and building this for vocational grads is **the strongest asset in
the whole plan**, not a liability to hide. Tradespeople are relentlessly marketed
to and can smell a growth funnel instantly. They are, however, genuinely warm
toward a young person trying to learn the industry and do something useful in it.

Every template here leads with that. The moment the copy starts sounding like a
startup pitch, it stops working.

## What we do not claim

Per the review, and worth restating because it is easy to drift:

- **No "AI-powered matching."** Not until there's enough activity for it to mean
  anything. There is no AI in the product today and the copy says so by omission.
- **No inflated numbers.** "I'm building this" beats "join thousands of pros" when
  the real number is 20. Anyone in the trades will check, and being caught
  inflating ends the relationship permanently.
- **No lead-gen framing.** We're upstream of Angie and TaskRabbit. Saying so is a
  differentiator; blurring it invites the comparison we lose.

## Success baseline

20–30 onboarded users, per the review. Worth tracking separately:

- Signups (vanity — easy to hit and easy to fool yourself with)
- **Members who posted or answered at least once** (the real number)
- Senior : junior ratio — a community of only juniors has nobody to learn from,
  and this is the metric most likely to quietly fail

If early feedback says people want the community and not the job board, the
review already agreed: trim it. Don't defend features because they're built.
