# Social accounts & first two weeks

Per the review: prioritise Instagram (reels and interview content), keep it
people-first, and don't expect the algorithm to do anything until posting is
consistent.

**Sequencing note:** most of this needs Krish, since it's his face and voice. The
account setup is the only part worth doing now — an empty account that sits idle
for three weeks is worse than one created the day you start posting, because
"posted twice in July, nothing since" is the first thing anyone checks.

## Bio

Same across platforms, under 150 characters:

> Free community for new plumbers, HVAC techs & electricians. Ask questions, find
> mentors, team up on jobs. Not a lead service. 🔧 home-fixr.com

The "not a lead service" line does real work — it's the first objection, answered
before it's raised.

**Put the link in the bio only once DNS is switched.** Until then use
`home-fixr.vercel.app`, which looks unpolished but works; a dead link in a bio is
worse.

## The content that actually works: interviews

The review landed on this and it's right. Short interviews with real tradespeople
do three things at once: they're credible content, they're a reason to contact
someone, and **the interviewee usually becomes a user.** The ask is "can I
interview you", not "please sign up" — a far easier yes that arrives at the same
place.

**Format:** 60–90 seconds, phone camera, vertical, one question per clip.

**Questions that produce good answers:**
- What do you wish someone had told you in your first year?
- What's the most expensive mistake you've made?
- How did you figure out what to charge?
- What's the thing trade school didn't teach you?
- When do you walk away from a job?
- What do you look for when you take on an apprentice?

**Where to find interviewees:** the same firm calls in
[firm-partnerships.md](firm-partnerships.md). Add one line: *"Would you be open to
a two-minute video answering one question for younger people entering the trade?"*
Many who decline signing up will say yes to this — and then sign up anyway.

**Always get explicit permission to post,** and send them the clip before it goes
up. One person feeling ambushed costs more than the post earns.

## First two weeks

Post 4–5 times a week. Cadence matters more than production quality — a shaky
phone clip posted consistently beats a polished one posted monthly.

Post 1 sets the tone for everything after it. It should sound like a seventeen
year old explaining a real problem, not a founder announcing a launch.

## Rules

- **Never fake activity.** No invented testimonials, no screenshots of
  conversations that didn't happen. Tradespeople talk to each other and this is a
  small world.
- **Never claim numbers you don't have.** "I'm building this" is a fine thing to
  say for months.
- **No AI claims.** Per the review — not until there's enough data for it to mean
  anything.
- **Reply to every comment for the first month**, including hostile ones. A
  measured reply to "this is pointless" is more persuasive to onlookers than the
  original post.
- **Show the product working**, not slideshows about it. Screen recordings of a
  real question getting a real answer outperform any graphic.

## Measuring it

Ignore follower count. Track:
- Profile → link clicks
- Signups attributable to social (ask "how did you find us?" in early
  conversations; there's no analytics in the product yet)
- **Interviews recorded** — the leading indicator, since each one is both content
  and a relationship

Ten interviews with real tradespeople is worth more than ten thousand followers,
and is the asset that makes the next twenty conversations easier.
